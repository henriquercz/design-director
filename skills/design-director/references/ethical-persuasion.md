# Ethical persuasion and behavioral design

Use behavioral mechanisms only to clarify value, reduce unnecessary cognitive effort, support deliberate progress, or make real consequences legible. Never use them to manufacture false urgency, hide costs, exploit compulsion, or obstruct exit.

## Allowed when truthful and reversible

- visual hierarchy that makes the intended primary action obvious;
- sensible defaults that help the user's stated goal and are easy to change;
- progress indicators tied to real progress;
- specific, verifiable social proof;
- real scarcity or capacity limits with accurate context;
- clear framing of tradeoffs and consequences;
- reminders of genuinely accumulated user-created value;
- reducing unnecessary choices when alternatives remain discoverable;
- post-action reassurance that accurately explains what happened and how to undo/recover.

## High-risk patterns

Treat these as requiring explicit ethical review and usually reject them:

- fake scarcity, fake countdowns, fabricated popularity, or unverifiable testimonials;
- preselected paid add-ons or consent-sensitive options;
- hiding total price, renewal terms, cancellation terms, or a materially cheaper/free option;
- obstructive cancellation, repeated guilt prompts, forced calls, or confusing reversal paths;
- decoys whose purpose is to deceive rather than clarify meaningful plan differences;
- artificially creating switching cost by withholding/export-blocking user data;
- variable-reward/compulsion loops designed to maximize time or spending against user interest;
- misleading precision, invented metrics, or fabricated evidence;
- dark-pattern defaults that benefit the product while predictably surprising the user.

## Default test

A default is defensible when all are true:

1. it is plausibly in the user's interest for the stated task;
2. the consequence is visible before commitment;
3. changing it is easy;
4. consent-sensitive or financial choices are not silently assumed;
5. the same choice would still feel acceptable if explained plainly to the user.

## Loss and urgency test

Only surface loss/urgency when the underlying condition is real and materially relevant. Prefer concrete consequence over emotional pressure.

Good: "Your draft will be deleted in 7 days" when that policy is real.
Bad: "Act now or miss out" with no real deadline.

## Retention test

Retention should come from recurring utility, learned workflow, personalization, collaboration, trustworthy history, and user-created value. Do not deliberately make exit harder than continued use.

## Ship gate

If a conversion or retention improvement depends on the user misunderstanding what will happen, it fails this skill even if the metric improves.
