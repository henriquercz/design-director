# Refinement moves

Refine changes the character of a design without pretending the brief changed. Choose one primary move based on diagnosis.

## Push
For safe/flat work. Increase commitment, contrast in hierarchy, specificity, point of view, or product clarity. "Bolder" is not "more effects."

## Settle
For loud/exhausting work. Reduce competing signals, decorative intensity, unnecessary motion, and hierarchy noise while retaining identity.

## Strip
For clutter. Remove elements that do not support the core task/evidence. Do not remove capability merely to make a screenshot cleaner.

## Proof
For brittle designs that fail with real data/states. Stress long strings, empty/huge lists, errors, slow networks, translations, permissions, overflow, and user mistakes.

## Activate
For first-time-use friction. Shorten the route to value, improve defaults, explain empty states, and make onboarding skippable/recoverable.

## Texture
For work that functions but has no memorable moments. Add tactile/visual character at moments that already carry emotion or meaning—completion, first-use, milestones, useful waiting, discovery—not every routine click.

## Push past limits
For surfaces whose value genuinely improves through technical ambition: rich visualization, cinematic narrative, extremely responsive search, high-performance large data, morphing continuity, generative visuals, etc. Name the benefit and risk first.

## Refine bar

- name the chosen move;
- apply it across the relevant surface, not one arbitrary detail;
- verify visible change in character/clarity/resilience/delight;
- preserve or improve the primary task;
- do not apply an opposite move immediately just to recover from overcorrection.
