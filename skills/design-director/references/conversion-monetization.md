# Conversion and monetization surfaces

Use for landing pages, signup/checkout, pricing, trials, plan comparison, upgrade prompts, and paid conversion.

## Context before cosmetics

Before changing CTA color, hero styling, or pricing-card decoration, establish:

- intended user/ICP in terms of trigger, need, constraints, and buying criteria;
- current awareness of the problem, solution category, and product;
- primary uncertainty/objection;
- proof available to answer that uncertainty;
- commitment required by the next action;
- source/context of traffic when known.

A visually strong page can still convert the wrong audience or promise the wrong thing.

## First-view comprehension

The first meaningful view should make it possible to infer quickly:

- what this is;
- who it is relevant to;
- what progress/result it enables;
- why this option is credible or meaningfully different;
- what the next step is.

Do not force every page into the same "problem-first" copy template; match message sequence to user awareness and category context.

## CTA contract

A primary CTA should communicate enough of the commitment that the user can predict the next step. Prefer specific action/outcome labels over generic "Submit", "OK", or contextless "Get started" when the object/action can be named.

Supporting microcopy may clarify:

- whether payment/card is required;
- expected time/effort;
- what happens immediately after click;
- trial/renewal terms;
- privacy or reversibility when material.

## Proof bar

Prefer proof that is specific, relevant to the intended user, and verifiable:

- product demonstration;
- before/after or benchmark with methodology/context;
- case study with concrete outcome;
- attributable testimonial;
- recognizable customer evidence when truthful;
- guarantees/commitments whose terms are visible.

Do not fabricate ratings, scarcity, logos, customer quotes, or metrics.

## Pricing architecture

Pricing should make the comparison model understandable:

- one or a small number of meaningful value axes;
- plan differences stated in user-relevant terms;
- total price, billing cadence, renewal, trial, and cancellation terms visible before commitment;
- a clearly selected/recommended plan only when the reason is defensible;
- no hidden downgrade/free path merely to force paid choice;
- no prechecked paid extras.

Good/Better/Best and anchoring are optional comparison structures, not universal laws.

## Upgrade timing

Prefer contextual upgrades when the user reaches a genuine usage/value boundary or explicitly seeks more capability. Avoid unrelated interruption. State:

- what is limited;
- what changes after upgrade;
- price/commitment;
- what happens if the user does not upgrade.

## Trial design

Trial length and payment requirements should match time-to-value, cost-to-serve, user intent, and business model. Do not assume "card required" or a fixed number of days is universally better. If a card is collected, terms and automatic conversion must be conspicuous and consent must be informed.

## Conversion quality

Optimize for useful, durable conversion rather than raw click/signup rate. Pair conversion with guardrails such as activation, qualified lead rate, refund/cancel rate, support burden, retained usage, and complaint rate when relevant.
